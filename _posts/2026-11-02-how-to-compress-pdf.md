Many application forms, job portals and government websites ask for a PDF under a certain size, such as 100 KB, 200 KB or 2 MB. A scanned certificate or a photo-heavy document can easily be 5 MB or more. This guide shows you how to shrink a PDF quickly, for free, and without uploading it anywhere.

## Step 1: Check what makes the file big

Most large PDFs are big for one of two reasons. Either the pages are high-resolution photos or scans, or the file contains extra data such as fonts, embedded images and metadata. Knowing which one applies helps you pick the right method.

## Step 2: Try the light method first

If your PDF is mostly text, a light compression is usually enough. Open our [Compress PDF tool](/tools/compress-pdf.html) and choose **Keep text**. This removes unnecessary data and keeps every word searchable. The file often becomes smaller without any visible change.

## Step 3: Use image compression for scans and photos

If the PDF is a scan or contains photos, the light method will not reduce it much. Choose **Smaller (images)** in the same tool, and pick **Medium** quality. Check the result. If the file is still too big, try **Low** quality. Remember that in this mode the text becomes part of an image, so it cannot be selected.

## Step 4: Compress the photos before making the PDF

If you are building a PDF from photos, compress each photo first with our [Image Compressor](/tools/image-compressor.html). Choose a maximum width of 800 or 1024 pixels, which is more than enough for most forms. Then combine them with [Image to PDF](/tools/image-to-pdf.html).

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Going too low too early.** Start with Medium quality. Go lower only if you need to.
- **Uploading the file to random websites.** Many free compressors upload your documents to their servers. Identity documents are better kept on your own device.
- **Ignoring the portal rules.** Some portals also limit the number of pages or the file type. Read the requirements before you start.

## Quick summary

Try Keep text first. If it is still big, use Smaller (images) with Medium quality. For best results, compress the photos before you combine them into a PDF.
